# Video script (2:51, 8 scenes)

Each row matches one scene of the video; the times are when the scene starts and ends. The final video uses the team's recordings: scene lengths and animation timing follow the recorded narration (`report/video_timing.json`), with synthesised background music (`scripts/make_music.py`) mixed under it by `scripts/mux_video_audio.py`.

| 1 | 0:00-0:13 | Title; boxes appear around each sign of a seal | The Indus script survives on about four thousand short inscriptions, four and a half thousand years old, and nobody can read it. We don't try to. We measure its structure, and how far each measurement can be trusted. |
| 2 | 0:13-0:31 | Counters; split bar; boxes forming on seals | We built a labelled set of 855 seal photographs with their sign sequences, and our team drew a box around every sign on 834 of them: more than four thousand signs. | 3 | 0:31-1:00 | A test seal cut into equal pieces, then hand boxes; accuracy bars | Milestone one reads a seal: find the text band, cut it into one piece per sign, and let a CNN name each piece. On this test seal the cuts slice the signs and the CNN gets two of six. With hand-drawn boxes, a CNN trained on them gets all six. Across all test seals: 33 percent from cuts, 74 percent from good boxes. So we trained a sign detector on our boxes, and it reads whole seals at 41 percent sign error without knowing the count. |
| 4 | 1:00-1:35 | Compositor: crops fly into a bank, stone is painted clean, signs are pasted along a real text; composites vs real; result bars | Professor Ashwin suggested we build new inscriptions out of the real signs we have. We cut every hand-boxed sign out of the training seals, painted the signs out of real seal surfaces, and pasted real signs along real corpus texts. Every composite comes with its exact sequence and boxes, for free. We made five thousand and trained a reader that needs no cutting, a CRNN with CTC loss. On a site it never saw, Harappa, sign error fell from 58 to 53 percent, and it got the sign count right on 65 seals instead of 53. |
| 5 | 1:35-1:57 | Jar sign bars; masked-prediction demo | Milestones two and three: the jar sign ends 35 percent of texts and almost never starts one, and the order is more predictable read right to left. Hide a sign, and bigram statistics recover it 35 percent of the time, 60 in the top five. A small transformer did worse: with under two thousand short texts, counting beats learning. |
| 6 | 1:57-2:16 | Guardrail bars | Milestone four: a Naive Bayes classifier tells real texts from shuffled ones 81 percent of the time. But Mesopotamian deity symbols, which are not writing, pass too. And a Markov chain with no language reproduces Zipf's law and the jar sign. Only groups of three signs set the Indus texts apart. |
| 7 | 2:16-2:41 | Error-propagation chart | Are our readings good enough for these statistics? With random mistakes, the structure washes out below about 60 percent accuracy, and our old 31 percent reader was not enough. Our new hand-box CNN, at 74 percent and with its own real mistakes, keeps every signal: real texts still beat shuffled ones 74 percent of the time. So the structure we measure is the script's, not the reader's. |
