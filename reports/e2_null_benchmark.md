# E2: null-model benchmark, what could a process with no language reproduce?

Real corpus: ICIT, 1837 texts, reading order. Each process generates corpora of the same size (20 per process; mean and range). Neither process has words, grammar or meaning.

## Corpus statistics

| Statistic | Real Indus corpus | Unigram process (no order) | Bigram process (neighbours only) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Distinct signs | 568 | 475 (463 to 498) | 476 (456 to 490) |
| Share of signs used only once | 38.6% | 23.6% (21.1% to 26.9%) | 23.2% (19.3% to 25.6%) |
| Mean text length | 4.55 | 4.55 (4.55 to 4.55) | 4.59 (4.44 to 4.66) |
| Zipf slope (top 100 signs) | -0.93 | -0.93 (-0.96 to -0.90) | -0.93 (-0.94 to -0.91) |
| Next-sign entropy, bits (B4 estimator) | 3.31 | 4.76 (4.73 to 4.79) | 3.00 (2.95 to 3.03) |
| Texts ending with the jar sign | 35.2% | 11.1% (9.9% to 12.5%) | 37.6% (35.1% to 39.9%) |

## Can a classifier tell real texts from generated ones? (held-out real texts, 5 runs; 50% = no)

| Generated by | Using sign pairs | Using sign triples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unigram process | 85.2% | 83.8% |
| Bigram process | 49.6% | 61.0% |
| Control: bigram process vs bigram process | 49.5% | 49.0% |

Here the bigram process is fitted on the training texts only, so the real test texts are new to it. The control row compares two generated corpora with each other and must sit at 50%.

## What this means

- Zipf's law, the jar sign at the end and next-sign entropy in this range are all reproduced by a process that only knows which sign follows which. On their own they cannot show that the script encodes language: a claim needs to beat this benchmark.
- Sign pairs cannot tell real texts from the bigram process (by construction). Sign triples can, above the control: real texts reuse three-sign combinations more than a pair-by-pair process does. That is structure beyond neighbours (recurring sign groups), and what M3 models beyond bigrams should capture.
- The real corpus has more rare signs (38.6% used once vs about 23%): a fitted process rarely draws a sign it saw once.
